# ADR 0004: Data amendments: leave-source-out calibration, a precise decontamination spec, and a release allow-list

- **Status:** accepted, 2026-11-22. **Amends** [ADR 0002](0002-data-licensing-and-decontamination.md); items not mentioned here stand.
- **Deciders:** @sourav-dhar
- **Evidence:**
  - [technical review](../research/reviews/01-technical-review.md) issues 11 and 21;
  - [architecture review](../research/reviews/03-architecture-review.md) §4.1 and §7;
  - [survey](../research/survey.md) §5.3 and §6.

## Context

The reviews found four gaps in ADR 0002:
- **Its calibration rule repeats a documented failure mode.**
  - Item 5 fits temperatures on held-out *items* of the training sources (DecisionBank by `group_id`, Nemotron by `doc_id`).
  - Kev lists fitting on held-out items of training sources as a failure mode (PLAN #5), and its in-distribution temperatures did not transfer to hard workloads ([survey](../research/survey.md) §5.3).
- **Its dedup rule cannot fire on short items, and its matching unit is undefined.**
  - A 13-gram overlap never matches an intent of about 10 tokens.
  - Option-only matches such as yes/no would flag everything.
- **Its registry misses suites we will report:**
  - JevAdvBench;
  - the kev-suites test splits;
  - documented overlaps with MMLU-Pro, CSQA, ARC, HellaSwag and BFCL v4;
  - the moving System1-Arena release.
- **It allows research-only data without saying what may go into a *released* checkpoint.** The sources' terms differ:
  - NVIDIA's text is "solely for the purpose of internal training";
  - DecisionBank says "do not use commercially";
  - kev-suites carries no licence;
  - OpenDecisions says "do not use for training".

  Separately, the CLM hard negatives we planned to replay were generated by Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite.

## Decision

1. **Calibration is fitted leave-source-out** (replaces item 5).
   - Temperatures are fitted on whole held-out *source datasets or task families*, then evaluated on different sources.
   - The fit sources are frozen before any model is scored.
   - $K$-conditioning is an experiment, not a default: global vs per-type vs per-type × $\log K$.
2. **Decontamination specification** (replaces item 3).
   - **Matching units:** the normalized state, the question, each option, and the (question, sorted option set) tuple.
   - **Option-only matches** on generic label sets (yes/no, true/false, numeric levels) are not used for removal.
   - **Short fields** (fewer than 20 tokens) are matched on character 5-gram shingles; long fields on word 13-grams.
   - **Fuzzy matching** uses MinHash with 128 permutations and Jaccard > 0.8.
   - **Long states** are also checked with word 8-gram Jaccard > 0.2 or containment ≥ 0.5, following Kev's rule.
   - **Label sets are hashed** to support a seen/unseen-label split.
3. **Registry additions** (extends item 2):
   - JevAdvBench;
   - the kev-suites test splits;
   - the MMLU-Pro, CSQA, ARC, HellaSwag and BFCL v4 items that the data notes link to training sources;
   - System1-Arena pinned to a dataset commit and a harness commit.
4. **Clean subsets.**
   - Every reported suite also reports a clean subset, with contaminated sources removed.
   - References whose training mixtures overlap a suite are flagged next to their scores.
5. **Release allow-list.**
   - A checkpoint we publish may only be trained on sources whose terms permit distributing trained weights.
   - Checkpoints trained on NVIDIA internal-training text, DecisionBank, kev-suites or other restricted sources stay research artifacts. They are reported, but not released.
   - OpenDecisions is never trained on.
6. **Closed-model provenance** (tightens item 6).
   - Hard negatives are mined with open models rather than replaying the Gemini-generated CLM negatives.
   - Unnamed teachers behind typed-decisions and DecisionBank's LLM-written rows are flagged on the model card.

## Consequences

- **Calibration numbers become transferable claims**, at the cost of fewer fitting sources.
- **The dedup filter needs fixtures** for short items and generic label sets. It runs in CI.
- **A public M3 release needs a separate allow-listed training run**, or it publishes only code and results.
