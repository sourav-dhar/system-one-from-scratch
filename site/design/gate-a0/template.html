Thirty-second summary

What it is

Qwen3-8B embeds the state-plus-question text and every option text separately. A state head and an action head project the 4096-d embeddings to 512-d unit vectors, and a softmax over cosines × 100 gives one probability per option.

CLM code: schema.py, heads.py, engine.py

Why it matters

An option's vector never depends on the state, so it is computed once and cached on the GPU, and state vectors are cached by their text too. A revisited state with cached options takes 0.6–0.7 ms on an RTX 4090 (1.7–2.0 ms without the GPU cache). A new state takes about 28 ms, mostly the backbone pass.

CLM README: server-side p50, fixed action set "mostly the backbone pass": our inference

Main weakness

The options never see the state, and the scale ships at its cap with no calibration. On System1-Arena CLM scores 41.4% with Brier 0.749, 17.8 points below an answer-token readout of its own backbone.

No experiment has isolated why. Rescaling cannot change which option wins, and our temperature fit moves CLM's Brier from 0.741 to 0.607 at unchanged accuracy. The benchmark also cut CLM's inputs to their last 2,048 tokens but not the readout's.

Architecture

Two towers share one frozen backbone. Only the two small heads and one scalar are trained: 18.9 million parameters on top of the frozen Qwen3-8B. Plate heights are proportional to layer width (4096 : 1536 : 512).

FrozenQwen3-8B, 36 decoder layers, used by both towers with the same weights. Never updated. Qwen3-8B config.json
TrainedThe state head, the action head (9,443,840 parameters each; 18.9 million together) and the logit scale λ. finetune.py, released checkpoint
CachedState and option projections, keyed by head and text, in one fixed GPU block (2% of memory by default, least recently used evicted). A repeat skips the backbone. cache.py, engine.py, README

The backbone's 4096-d last-token state is L2-normalised before the heads. Each head: Linear 4096→1536, GELU; Linear 1536→1536, LayerNorm, GELU; Linear 1536→512; then L2 normalisation, so the dot product of two outputs is a cosine. heads.py, released checkpoint

RouterBench items where CLM's top pick is GPT-3.5 Turbo or Claude 2 our run

On all 250 RouterBench items in System1-Arena, CLM's top pick is one of two options: Mixtral (196 items) or GPT-4 Turbo (54). It never picks GPT-3.5 Turbo, although that is the gold answer on 56 items. Its accuracy here is 51.6%, below the 60.8% of always answering Mixtral (152 of 250 gold labels).

The four options sit very close together. Inverting the softmax (scale 100, T = 1 in our port) recovers each option's cosine relative to the top one. On this item they span 0.021; across all 250 items the span runs from 0.011 to 0.027 (median 0.018). The ×100 scale turns this item's largest gap, 0.0214, into a probability ratio of 8.5. our run, exact arithmetic

Issue #15 reports that a shared question suffix seems to dominate last-token pooling. Here all 250 items share one 75-token routing instruction, appended to states of 62–467 tokens (median 124). That could explain why the top pick barely changes from item to item; it does not explain the small gaps within an item. Issue #3's short-label effect is less likely to apply, because these options are full descriptions, though their shared template could itself pull them together. None of this is tested here. our inference token counts: our run, Qwen3-8B tokenizer
